Masonry veneer: follow the changed opening

Learn to identify the wall route, calculate height above adjacent finished ground and apply the two conditions in the small-opening exception. Allow about 12 minutes.

The owner asks for a little more daylight. A 450 mm opening becomes 600 mm. The frame supplier updates its drawing, the window quote changes, and the bricklayer is handed the same elevation as last week.

That is the moment to stop and bring the documents together. In the NCC 2022 masonry veneer provisions, the width of an opening affects the steel lintel requirement. A lintel is the member spanning an opening to support the masonry above it. A change that looks small in a showroom can alter what needs to be ordered and built.

First, identify the wall

Section 5 divides masonry into different construction systems. Part 5.2 deals with masonry veneer, Part 5.3 with cavity masonry, Part 5.4 with single leaf unreinforced masonry and Part 5.5 with isolated piers. Components and accessories sit in Part 5.6, with weatherproofing in Part 5.7. Reinforced masonry has its own route through Volume Two, H1D5(4).

Find the wall type on the construction documents before choosing a detail. A brick exterior by itself does not identify which route applies. For a homeowner, this is a useful question for the designer. For a builder or trade, it belongs beside the wall schedule and current drawing revision.

Check the route before using the dimensions

Clauses 5.1.2 and 5.2.1 connect this work to Volume Two. The Part 5.2 route is subject to H1D5(1)(c), while compliance with H1D5(1)(a) or (b) provides alternative routes. Those conditions need to be checked for the actual project before using the construction provisions. State and territory variations also apply.

The Part 5.2 pathway has boundaries. H1D5(1)(c) requires a wind class no higher than N3 and site soil class A, S or M. It also requires compliant supporting footings or slabs, framing and masonry components, excludes alpine areas, and applies only where Appendix A of AS 1170.4 has no specific earthquake design requirements for the building. Check the whole list with the designer, not just the wind rating. H1D5(1)(a) and (b) provide the AS 3700 and AS 4773.1 plus AS 4773.2 alternatives.

This lesson uses the national NCC 2022 text dated 1 May 2023 for Class 1 and 10 buildings. Confirm the approval edition, applicable amendments and state or territory variations with the project team. It is general learning, not a project-specific design, compliance certificate or accredited CPD.

Measure height from the ground beside the wall

Clause 5.2.2 limits masonry veneer to 8.5 m above adjacent finished ground level. The word “finished” matters when landscaping levels change. On sloping ground, check the relevant wall elevations against the ground beside them. A height written against the highest ground point can leave the lower side unchecked.

For example, if the top of a wall is at level 108.50 and the adjacent finished ground is at 100.00, the height is 8.50 m. If that ground level changes to 99.80, the height becomes 8.70 m. Under this route, that changed arrangement exceeds the stated limit. Resolve it with the designer before work proceeds.

Follow the opening through to its support

Under 5.2.3, masonry veneer openings generally require steel lintels. The exception covers openings no wider than 500 mm, and it also requires adequate support. Both conditions matter.

Our fictional 450 mm opening could only use that exception after adequate support had been established. At 600 mm, it exceeds the width allowed by the exception. The updated documents need to show the steel lintel arrangement under this route. Clause 5.2.3 alone does not specify a complete lintel selection. The supporting design and applicable accessory provisions still need to be resolved.

Ask for the revised opening dimension, the nominated support detail, the drawing revision and confirmation that the ordered materials match. Carry the change through the frame, window and masonry documents together.

A useful pre-start conversation

Before the bricklayer starts, pick one changed opening and trace it across the elevation, wall schedule, frame drawing and window order. Record the wall type, clear opening, supporting detail and revision. Give any mismatch an owner and a response date.

Homeowners can help by confirming selections early and asking for written confirmation of the agreed change. Builders can use the same record to make sure each trade is pricing and building the same arrangement. Five minutes spent on that conversation can prevent a very awkward delivery day.

Last week we followed a revised bathroom recess into the slab reinforcement. The habit carries into masonry: follow the changed dimension all the way to what supports it. Read Five millimetres deeper.

Put the documents together

Fictional document desk

  • Elevation revision C: window W04, 600 mm opening.
  • Window order revision B: W04, 450 mm.
  • Frame drawing revision C: W04, 600 mm.
  • Masonry elevation revision B: W04, 450 mm; support not confirmed.
Compare with an example response

W04 is 600 mm on elevation and frame revision C but 450 mm on the window order and masonry elevation revision B. Please have the designer confirm the opening and support detail, then issue coordinated revisions to the window supplier, frame supplier and bricklayer before manufacture or construction of this opening. Record who owns the response and the agreed date.

A useful query identifies the conflicting revisions, asks for a resolved support detail, and names the affected documents. It does not choose a lintel from the opening width alone.
