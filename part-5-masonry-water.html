1. Start with the water route

The brick face is the visible finish. Behind it, the cavity, flashing and outlets have different jobs. A gap in a joint is not proof that the whole route works. Trace where water is collected, how it reaches the flashing and where it can leave the wall.

Clause 5.2.4 takes masonry veneer to 5.7.3 and 5.7.4 for damp-proof courses and flashings. Read those with the cavity and weephole provisions in 5.7.2 and 5.7.5. That is the grouped detail in this lesson.

This lesson teaches the national NCC 2022 Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023, focused on masonry veneer. Confirm the project’s approval edition, amendments and state or territory variations. Part 5.7 is subject to H2D4(2)(c); H2D4(2)(a) and (b) provide alternative routes. The structural masonry route must also be established. This is general education, not project certification or accredited CPD.

2. A cavity is a clear space

Under 5.7.2, the masonry-veneer cavity is 25 to 75 mm clear. The separate cavity-masonry range is 35 to 75 mm. Do not swap the two wall systems. The explanatory information says the veneer minimum must still be maintained where membranes, sheet bracing or services are installed.

For slab-on-ground construction, the cavity drains outside through the 5.7.5 arrangement. The exterior leaf’s overhang at the slab edge is limited to 15 mm. Where mullions occupy the cavity, the explanatory information calls for a vertical damp-proof course between mullion and outer leaf.

Fictional check: a nominal 40 mm space has a 17 mm projection into it. Only 23 mm remains. The original drawing dimension does not establish the as-built clearance.

3. Identify the material before it disappears

Clause 5.7.3 provides material pathways, including AS/NZS 2904-compliant material and specified polyethylene, coated-metal and bituminous alternatives. A roll’s colour alone proves none of them.

For example, the embossed polyethylene option requires high impact resistance, low slip, nominal thickness of 0.5 mm before embossing and compliance with clause 7.6 of AS/NZS 2904. The termite-sheet option has its own Part 3.4, no-penetration and continuity conditions. Do not assume any termite product is also suitable flashing.

Use product identification and the relevant supporting documentation. The referenced Australian Standard is not reproduced in this lesson. Read all five material alternatives in 5.7.3.

4. Follow the flashing past the opening

Clause 5.7.4 requires continuity at the locations it lists, including wall bases, suspended-floor walls and piers, roof intersections, and openings. The barrier is continuous through the wall or pier and visible at the outside face.

For sill and head flashings, 5.7.4(3) includes at least 150 mm extension beyond each reveal, a sill position no more than one course below the sill brick course, and a head position no more than 300 mm above the opening. It also specifies the cavity turn-up, at least 30 mm embedment into the relevant masonry leaf and attachment to the window or wall framing. Read the complete arrangement, not a single measurement.

The head-flashing exception in 5.7.4(1)(a)(v) requires an eave wider than three times the veneer height above the opening. Equality does not meet “more than”. This exception does not remove every flashing requirement.

5. Finished levels change the check

The standard adjacent-ground case in 5.7.4(2) requires a DPC at least 150 mm above ground. The 75 mm option is tied to finished paved, concreted or landscaped areas sloping away from the wall. A separate 50 mm option also requires the cited drainage provision and protection from direct weather. Low-rainfall-intensity provisions are further conditional alternatives, not a blanket allowance to reduce clearance.

Fictional change: DPC elevation 10.300 m, adjacent garden ground initially 10.140 m. The separation is 160 mm. Raising that ground to 10.180 m reduces it to 120 mm. The unchanged brickwork now fails the 150 mm criterion in this selected scenario. This does not select a remediation or assess every requirement.

6. Weepholes and their exceptions

Clause 5.7.5 locates open perpend weepholes in the course immediately above flashing, including a DPC serving as flashing. The openings are at least 50 mm high, the width of the vertical mortar joint, with centres no more than 1.2 m apart.

The clause excludes head openings less than 1.2 m wide, locations beneath window and door sills, and the specified elevated external impervious surface for H8P1 step-free access. At exactly 1.2 m, the small-head-opening exception does not apply. An exception to weepholes is not an exception to the separate flashing rules.

Before concealing the work, coordinate the current detail, material record, finished levels and photographs. This evidence routine is Build Compass practice guidance, not an additional NCC clause.
