Support the tiles and the water beneath

Tiles shed most rain. Sarking and anti-ponding details deal with the water that reaches the layer underneath.

Coverage: 7.3.1–7.3.6: tile fixings, flashings, sarking and discharge

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 10

1. Select the tiled-roof pathway

The tiled-roof provisions sit under H1D7 and distinguish product, pitch and support requirements. Tile shape and material alone do not establish suitability for a roof. Confirm the product and roof geometry, then coordinate battens and support with the framing design rather than transferring a sheet-roof arrangement.

Read with the source

7.3.1–7.3.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 157. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Match the tile specification to the roof plan and framing route.

2. Wind affects tile fixing

Tile fixing is selected from the wind-classification table. Field tiles, perimeter tiles and ridges or hips do not necessarily use the same pattern. The table identifies nails, screws, clips and compliant adhesive systems. Bedding alone must not be assumed to satisfy the nominated mechanical or adhesive fixing requirement.

Read with the source

7.3.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 157. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Mark the edge zones and field pattern before installation begins.

3. Use the actual fixing specification

The listed fixing options include nails with a minimum 2.8 mm diameter and 15 mm penetration, or the specified screw arrangement with 15 mm penetration. Purpose-made clips and the referenced flexible pointing route are separate options. A shorter fixing is not equivalent merely because it holds a tile during installation.

Read with the source

7.3.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 157. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check penetration into the intended support, not the overall fixing length alone.

4. Know when sarking is required

Sarking is required beneath all tiles below 18 degrees. At higher pitches, the table also limits rafter length without sarking: 4500 mm from 18 to less than 20 degrees, 5500 mm from 20 to less than 22, and 6000 mm at 22 degrees or more. These lengths are measured down from the apex. Where the maximum length is exceeded, the table note requires sarking over the remainder towards the eave line, or equivalent where there are no eaves. Other applicable requirements may still call for sarking.

Read with the source

7.3.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 163. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check pitch and rafter length together.

5. Install a continuous drainage layer

Sarking must comply with the specified material standard and be installed with at least 150 mm overlaps or taped joints. Support and fixing keep the layer in place, with sag limited by the provision. Torn or obstructed sarking can interrupt the secondary water path even beneath an otherwise neat tiled finish.

Read with the source

7.3.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 163. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Inspect the underlay before the remaining tiles hide it.

6. Prevent water pooling at the eaves

Where sarking is installed, an anti-ponding device or board is required on roofs below 20 degrees and on roofs with no eaves overhang regardless of pitch. Both cases depend on sarking being installed. The board must be water resistant and arranged to carry the sarking towards discharge. Do not create a pocket behind the fascia that holds water against the underlay.

Read with the source

7.3.5. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 164. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the eaves section, not just the roof plan.

7. Flash the penetration and discharge to the gutter

Tile flashings must follow the roof shape and drain the area above a penetration. The national penetration detail uses at least 75 mm upturn and 150 mm surrounding width moulded into the tiles, with flow-directed laps. Where the specified eaves gutter is provided, tiles must overhang the fascia or tiling batten by at least 35 mm.

7.3.3; 7.3.6. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 160. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the junction and actual overhang before closing access.

9. Check your understanding

1. Can a sheet-roof support layout be adopted without checking for tiles?

2. Does the field fixing pattern automatically apply at every edge?

3. Is overall nail length the same as embedment?

4. Does a pitch above 18 degrees always remove the need for sarking?

5. Can a torn layer be accepted because the tiles are watertight-looking?

6. Does a roof with sarking but without eaves escape the anti-ponding requirement at a steeper pitch?

7. Does a 20 mm tile overhang satisfy the stated 35 mm minimum?

Practice: A 19 degree roof has a 4800 mm rafter. By how many mm does it exceed the 4500 mm table limit without sarking?
Worked answer: 300. 4800 − 4500 = 300 mm. Under this table the no-sarking case is exceeded.
