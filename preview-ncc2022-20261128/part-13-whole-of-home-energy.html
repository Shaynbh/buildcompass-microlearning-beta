Count the services as well as the shell

The building fabric is only one part of energy use. The whole-of-home method also accounts for equipment, pumps and on-site generation.

Coverage: 13.6.1–13.6.2

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 9

1. Separate fabric from whole-of-home

Part 13.6 applies through H6D2(2) to the stated Class 1 and conditioned Class 10a buildings. It is separate from the fabric and glazing checks. The source flags NSW, NT and Tasmania variations. A good envelope result is not automatically a completed whole-of-home assessment.

Read with the source

13.6.1. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 403. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Keep both assessment routes and their inputs in the project record.

2. Use the defined floor area

The floor-area adjustment uses area measured within the inside faces of the external walls and includes the stated conditioned attached Class 10a area. The table notes address rounding where values lie between ranges. The gross footprint used for a sales brochure may not be the required assessment area.

Read with the source

13.6.2; Table 13.6.2a. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 403. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record the measured area and selected factor band.

3. Select the equipment efficiency factors

The method draws the main space-conditioning and water-heater efficiency factor from the ABCB Standard for Whole-of-Home Efficiency Factors. The actual equipment choice matters. Do not replace that factor with a retail star label or guessed annual bill; use the referenced method and retain the selected equipment evidence.

Read with the source

13.6.2(1). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 403. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Keep the exact factor source with the equipment schedule.

4. Include pool and spa pumps

Pool and spa pump contributions use their separate volume rounding and factor tables. Pool volume is taken to the nearest 1000 litres and spa volume to the nearest 100 litres. Pump rating and jurisdiction affect the relevant entries. Omitting an installed pump changes the assessment inputs.

Read with the source

13.6.2(2)–(3). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 403. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the landscape and pool schedule alongside the house services.

5. Use the actual photovoltaic capacity

Installed on-site photovoltaic capacity is a defined input in the method. A future intention to add panels is not the same as the documented installed capacity. Coordinate the electrical design and final equipment schedule so the assessment does not rely on a system that disappears during procurement.

Read with the source

13.6.2(1). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 403. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record the final installed capacity and any substitutions.

6. Compare against the correct allowance

The allowance uses the stated floor-area and energy factors, including climate and jurisdiction. Keep the full equation and its units in the approved calculation. This lesson explains the inputs but does not present a simplified score as a compliance result; changing equipment requires the supported calculation to be updated.
