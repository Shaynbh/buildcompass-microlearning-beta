Select the member and its connections
NCC 2022 Housing Provisions national text, 1 May 2023. Review draft.
Coverage: 6.3.1–6.3.9

1. Use the right application: The structural-steel provisions operate within H1D6 and their stated limitations. Bearers, strutting beams, lintels and columns use different tables. A beam that looks similar to a table section is not sufficient evidence of the required steel grade, support conditions or load arrangement.
Source: 6.3.1–6.3.2

2. Bearer load width matters: Bearer tables distinguish single and continuous spans and timber and tiled floors. Effective spacing comes from the referenced framing pathway. The continuous-span provisions limit the difference between spans to 10%. Table notes describe the assumed loads and restraints, so the longest printed span cannot be used without those conditions.
Source: 6.3.3

3. Strutting loads are positioned: Strutting-beam tables separate sheet and tiled roofs. Their point load must act within the middle third of the span. Effective load width, roof tie-down, connections and supporting columns remain part of the selection. Moving the strut towards an end changes the assumed arrangement even when the beam length stays the same.
Source: 6.3.4

4. Lintels need restraint: Steel lintel tables distinguish roof covering and effective load width. Loading is taken as evenly distributed and the top flange is laterally restrained at load points. Lintels used with masonry must also satisfy the relevant masonry pathway. A correct span with an unrestrained top flange is not the stated table case.
Source: 6.3.5

5. Columns, bearing and connections: Columns are selected for supported area, height and roof or floor loads, and require restraint in two orthogonal directions. Member bearing is at least the member width at a single bearing and twice that width at an internal continuous support. Connection figures and specified corrosion protection for bolts remain part of the detail.
Source: 6.3.6–6.3.7

6. Resolve holes and coating damage: Permitted penetrations are governed by the original member-zone figures. Do not drill a convenient hole based on a generic rule about the centre of a beam. Protective coatings depend on exposure, and cuts or welds require repair. Coordinate services before fabrication and retain the agreed structural and coating details.
Source: 6.3.8–6.3.9

Practice: A 100 mm wide member uses the stated internal continuous-support bearing rule. What is the minimum bearing length in mm?
Worked answer: 200. 2 × 100 = 200 mm. All other table, support and connection conditions remain applicable.

Further study: Original span tables, connection and penetration figures require table-by-table visual verification; this lesson teaches the selection process and critical conditions, not an automated steel design.
