Build the waterproof layer before the finish
NCC 2022 Housing Provisions national text, 1 May 2023. Review draft.
Coverage: 10.1; 10.2.1–10.2.32: extent, materials and connected details

1. Map waterproof and water-resistant areas: Shower areas, baths, vessels and sanitary compartments have different required extents. Waterproof and water-resistant are different requirements. Floor substrate also matters: outside showers, timber-based floors have different treatment from the listed concrete or fibre-cement cases. Draw the required extents before choosing the membrane system.
Before applying these details, identify the relevant Volume Two pathway and read the state or territory schedule. Scope clauses identify the subjects covered; they do not make every detail applicable to every building. Use the defined terms and original figures where the provision depends on geometry.
Source: 10.1.1–10.1.2; 10.2.1–10.2.5

2. Select compatible materials: Waterproof and water-resistant systems depend on the specified material combinations. A water-resistant sheet is not automatically a complete waterproof wall. Membranes, substrates, sealants, primers and drainage components must form a compatible installed system. Product substitution needs to preserve the nominated performance rather than simply use another product of a similar colour.
Source: 10.2.6–10.2.11

3. Coordinate levels, falls and shower type: Where a floor waste is installed, the national provision requires continuous falls between 1:80 and 1:50. Enclosed and unenclosed showers, hobs, step-downs and level thresholds use different details. A timber hob is not one of the listed hob materials. Plan the substrate, screed, tiles and waterstop together before setting drain heights.
Source: 10.2.12–10.2.20

4. Keep the membrane continuous at movement: Substrates must be prepared and penetrations sealed using the applicable details. Bond breakers are required at the specified wall, floor, hob and movement junctions and must suit the membrane flexibility class. The shower membrane extent in 10.2.25 is measured from the finished tile level, so do not confuse it with an earlier substrate-based dimension.
Source: 10.2.21–10.2.28

5. Join the membrane to the drainage: The waterproof layer must terminate into or at a suitable drainage flange to create a waterproof connection under the applicable floor arrangement. Preformed shower bases also need drainage of the tile bed and a waterproof riser connection. A floor waste at the right height does not prove the hidden membrane-to-drain joint is complete.
Source: 10.2.29–10.2.30

6. Finish doors and thresholds carefully: Perimeter and doorway waterstops must connect with the floor membrane and required flashings. Door-frame portions below tile level need the specified protection. These details must be coordinated with architraves and later fixing work so that the completed waterproof boundary is not punctured or bypassed by moisture-sensitive material.
Source: 10.2.24; 10.2.31–10.2.32

Practice: At a fall of 1:80 over 1600 mm, what is the vertical fall in mm?
Worked answer: 20. 1600 ÷ 80 = 20 mm. Confirm the actual floor-waste provision and complete finished-floor geometry.

Further study: Full bath, bidet, shower-screen and all threshold figures require final clause-by-clause verification. The module is a connected-system lesson, not certification of a waterproofing installation.
