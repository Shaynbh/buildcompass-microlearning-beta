Keep the exit usable in snow

Snow changes how an entrance works. Alpine details supplement structural, energy and safe-movement requirements.

Coverage: 12.1.1–12.1.2; 12.2.1–12.2.4

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 9

1. Confirm the alpine application

Part 12.2 applies through H7D3 and the defined alpine-area scope. It does not replace the structural actions, energy-efficiency or general stair provisions. The other ancillary topics, including bushfire, flood and swimming pools, have Volume Two pathways outside this Part. Establish the location and governing route first.

Before applying these details, identify the relevant Volume Two pathway and read the state or territory schedule. Scope clauses identify the subjects covered; they do not make every detail applicable to every building. Use the defined terms and original figures where the provision depends on geometry.

2. Let doors open despite snow

An external door subject to snow build-up must open inwards or slide, with its threshold at least 900 mm above the adjoining surface under this detail. Class 1b also requires the specified inward-opening sign. Coordinate the approach, landing and door operation rather than treating the threshold as an isolated dimension.

Read with the source

12.2.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 314. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the whole access section at the doorway.

3. Keep the external egress surface open

External trafficable structures used for egress require the specified expanded-mesh floor surface. Required barriers have sides at least 75% open. These details reduce snow accumulation while retaining the separate fall-protection and structural requirements. An open-looking decorative pattern is not proof of the required open proportion.

Read with the source

12.2.3(a)–(b). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 314. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record the open-area calculation and barrier design together.

4. Use the alpine ramp and stair options

An alpine ramp serving an external doorway must not be steeper than 1:12. Stairs use either the referenced general table or the alpine alternative table. The alternative has its own riser, going and slope-relationship limits; choosing a shallower riser alone does not establish the complete arrangement.

Read with the source

12.2.3(c)–(d). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 314. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the selected table as a whole.

5. Allow snow space beside taller walls

For external-wall portions more than 3.6 m above natural ground, the boundary distance is at least 2.5 m plus 100 mm for each 300 mm or part above 3.6 m. The relevant boundary exclusion still applies. Round an incomplete increment upward rather than treating it as no additional height.

Read with the source

12.2.4(a). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 315. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record natural ground, wall height and the rounded increment count.

6. Avoid trapping snow at the exit

Doorways into vehicle-access courts between wings require the specified 4 m clear distance. A doorway facing a snow-trapping feature or sufficiently high embankment also needs the stated clearance. Review the landscape and neighbouring features as well as the building footprint because later works can change the exit condition.

Practice: A relevant wall portion is 4.0 m above natural ground. How many 300 mm increments or parts lie above 3.6 m?
