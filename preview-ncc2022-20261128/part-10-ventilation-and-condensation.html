Move moist air outside

An exhaust fan needs a discharge route and replacement air. Wall and roof layers also need a deliberate moisture strategy.

Coverage: 10.6.1–10.6.3; 10.8.1–10.8.3

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 9

1. Choose the room ventilation route

The natural-ventilation route uses openings with a ventilating area at least 5% of the room floor area, opening to the stated outdoor or adjoining space. Mechanical alternatives apply under their specified conditions. Do not confuse the light-transmitting area of a fixed pane with its ability to ventilate a room.

Read with the source

10.6.1–10.6.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 271. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record the route and opening arrangement for each room.

2. Borrow air only under the conditions

Borrowed ventilation requires the internal opening and the external opening for the combined room areas to satisfy the provision. Neither room can be a sanitary compartment under this borrowed-air route. A sanitary compartment opening directly into a kitchen or pantry needs the specified separation or mechanical-exhaust arrangement.

Read with the source

10.6.2–10.6.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 271. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check room relationships, not just total window size.

3. Match membrane permeance to climate

External wall membranes must follow the material and installation standards and sit outside the primary insulation layer. The national minimum vapour permeance is 0.143 μg/N.s in climate zones 4 and 5, and 1.14 in zones 6–8. Water resistance and vapour permeability describe different behaviour; a foil face is not automatically suitable.

Read with the source

10.8.1. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 285. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the product declaration and the complete wall build-up.

4. Discharge exhaust outdoors

The minimum exhaust flow is 25 L/s for a bathroom or sanitary compartment and 40 L/s for a kitchen or laundry. Exhaust must discharge to outdoor air, directly or through a shaft or duct. Venting clothes dryers also discharge outdoors. Emptying a duct into the roof space is not that arrangement.

Read with the source

10.8.2(1)–(3). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 285. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Trace the duct to its actual external termination.

5. Provide run-on and make-up air

A non-continuous bathroom or toilet exhaust serving a room without the specified natural ventilation must be interlocked with the light and run for 10 minutes after switch-off. The relevant rooms also need make-up air, using the 14,000 mm² free-opening option or AS 1668.2. Dryer make-up air has its own referenced route.

Read with the source

10.8.2(4)–(6). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 285. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the door undercut or alternative supply path after floor finishes.

6. Vent the roof space where required

In climate zones 6–8, the national roof-space provision specifies location above the insulation or qualifying layer, at least 20 mm height and the relevant ventilation arrangement. Opening areas depend on pitch, with an additional eaves requirement for cathedral ceilings. Concrete roofs, structural insulated panels and BAL-FZ roofs have stated exceptions.

Read with the source

10.8.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 286. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Use the pitch row and verify both low- and high-level openings where required.

Practice: A door undercut is 20 mm high and 700 mm wide. What unobstructed opening area in mm² does that rectangle provide?
