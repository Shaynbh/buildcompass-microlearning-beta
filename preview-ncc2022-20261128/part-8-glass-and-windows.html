Choose glass for wind, impact and use

A window can resist wind and still need different glass because someone can walk into it. Keep those checks separate.

Coverage: 8.1.1–8.1.2; 8.2.1–8.2.2; 8.3.1–8.3.3; 8.4.1–8.4.8

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 9

1. Assembly and pane are different checks

Section 8 separates window assemblies from glass selection. Its simplified provisions exclude particular arrangements, including several non-vertical, frameless and special-purpose installations. Identify the assembly pathway and its scope before selecting pane thickness. A glass invoice does not establish performance of the whole frame and fixing system.

Before applying these details, identify the relevant Volume Two pathway and read the state or territory schedule. Scope clauses identify the subjects covered; they do not make every detail applicable to every building. Use the defined terms and original figures where the provision depends on geometry.

On the job

Retain both assembly and glazing evidence.

2. Leave room above the frame

Windows must not carry building loads. Installation includes packing and fixing that keep the frame straight, with at least a 10 mm gap above the frame under this detail. Wider openings or structural movement may require more allowance. Do not bridge the head gap with rigid material that transfers unintended loads.

Read with the source

8.2.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 189. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Coordinate the head gap with lintel movement and flashing.

3. Select glass for the actual panel

The glass tables distinguish type, thickness, panel dimensions and wind classification within their scope. Fully framed support is an important condition. A larger pane may leave the selected table entry even if the window opening stays in the same wall. Human-impact requirements must then be checked separately.

Read with the source

8.3.2–8.3.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 191. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record panel dimensions rather than just overall window size.

4. Recognise doors and side panels

Doors, nearby side panels and glazing that could be mistaken for an opening have specific safety-glass requirements and limited exceptions. For side panels the nearest sight line within 300 mm of the door is important. Do not assume an adjacent fixed panel is harmless because it does not open.

Read with the source

8.4.2–8.4.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 194. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Mark pedestrian approach routes on the glazing schedule.

5. Check low glass and wet areas

Low-level glazing and glazing in kitchens, bathrooms and spa areas use different trigger heights and exceptions. The wet-area provision addresses glazing below 2 m and distinguishes framed glass from exposed edges. A general low-window rule is not a substitute for checking the wet-area location and the specific permitted exceptions.

Read with the source

8.4.5–8.4.6. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 196. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Identify the room, sill height and whether an edge is exposed.

6. Make glass visible and identifiable

Where required, a visible opaque band helps people recognise glazing, but it does not replace the required safety-glass grade. Safety-glass identification records the relevant standard and product information. Check both visibility and evidence before handover rather than relying on the assumption that all new glass is safety glass.

Read with the source

8.4.7–8.4.8. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 198. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Retain identification evidence and check any required visibility treatment.

Practice: The nearest side-panel sight line is 250 mm from a door. How far inside the 300 mm side-panel threshold is it?
Worked answer: 50. 300 − 250 = 50 mm. Apply the full side-panel provision and its exceptions to the actual panel.
