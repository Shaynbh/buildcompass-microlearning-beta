Give heavy rain a safe way out

A gutter can collect ordinary rain and still fail in a downpour. Capacity and overflow need separate checks.

Coverage: 7.4.1–7.4.7

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 10

1. Check the drainage route

Part 7.4 provides a limited eaves-gutter and downpipe pathway under H2D6. Other drainage arrangements use the applicable alternative, including AS/NZS 3500. Do not treat the eaves-gutter tables as a box-gutter design. Start with roof catchments, rainfall location and a clearly identified discharge arrangement.

Read with the source

7.4.1. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 166. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Label box gutters and non-standard arrangements for the correct design route.

2. Use two rainfall checks

Gutter and downpipe sizing uses the five-minute rainfall intensity for a 5% annual exceedance probability. Overflow is assessed using the 1% event. Those are different design inputs. A completed ordinary-flow calculation is not evidence that an overflowing gutter will keep water out of the building.

Read with the source

7.4.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 167. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Keep normal drainage and overflow calculations as separate records.

3. Install fall and support

Eaves gutters need a fall of at least 1:500 and supporting brackets at ends, corners and the specified maximum spacing of 1.2 m. Drainage capacity also depends on the selected gutter and outlet. Flat spots or sagging sections can defeat a design that was adequate on paper.

Read with the source

7.4.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 171. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check levels after brackets and roofing are installed.

4. Place downpipes with the catchment

A downpipe must not serve more than 12 m of gutter under this arrangement and should be located close to valley discharge where practicable. Its size follows the applicable table and roof catchment. The 12 m limit is not a substitute for checking the area feeding that length of gutter.

Read with the source

7.4.5. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 173. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Map which roof planes feed each outlet.

5. Continuous overflow has defined details

Continuous overflow options include slots, a controlled gap behind the gutter and the specified front-bead relationship to the fascia. Their capacity depends on exact geometry, not simply the presence of holes. For example, the controlled-back-gap option specifies a minimum gap and spacer arrangement as well as gutter-back height.

Read with the source

7.4.6. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 173. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Measure the selected complete option and record its stated capacity.

6. Dedicated outlets have their own limits

End-stop, inverted-nozzle, front-weir and rainhead overflow arrangements each have defined dimensions and capacities. Their location must discharge safely rather than direct water into the wall. Do not add the capacity of an imagined opening or assume that a downpipe itself is a dedicated overflow device.

Read with the source

7.4.7. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 174. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Show where overflow water goes at the completed building.

7. Select compatible drainage materials

Gutters and downpipes must meet the listed material and product requirements. Compatibility also matters at roof and flashing interfaces, including runoff from upstream materials. Lead is not suitable where the roof water is collected for drinking. Select the drainage material together with the roof covering instead of treating the gutter as an isolated finish choice.

Read with the source

7.4.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 167. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check roof, flashing, gutter and downpipe material records together.

9. Check your understanding

1. Can the eaves-gutter table alone establish a box-gutter design?

2. Does passing the 5% event establish overflow adequacy?

3. Is nominal fall on a drawing enough if the installed gutter sags?

4. Can every 12 m gutter run use any downpipe size?

5. Does any gap behind a gutter establish the listed overflow capacity?

6. Is a normal downpipe automatically a separate overflow outlet?

7. Can drinking-water collection ignore a lead flashing upstream?

Practice: At a minimum fall of 1:500, how much drop in mm is needed over a 10,000 mm gutter run?
Worked answer: 20. 10,000 ÷ 500 = 20 mm. Outlet, capacity, overflow and installation checks remain separate.
