Balance winter heat and summer shade

A good glazing choice depends on the room, orientation, frame and shade. Double glazing is a description, not a complete energy specification.

Coverage: 13.3.1–13.3.4

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 9

1. Use the correct assessment route

The national Part 13.3 route applies through H6D2(1)(b) to the stated buildings. NSW, NT and Tasmania have flagged variations. Do not transplant one table result into a different assessment method or jurisdiction. Keep the nominated route visible when discussing a glazing substitution.

Read with the source

13.3.1. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 369. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Match the product schedule to the approved assessment method.

2. Use whole-system performance

The calculations use total-system U-value and SHGC, covering the combined glass and frame. A centre-of-glass value is not the same input. U-value describes heat transfer, while SHGC describes solar heat gain. A product can improve one measure while changing the other, so compare both against the selected design.

Read with the source

13.3.2–13.3.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 369. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Ask for representative whole-window performance evidence.

3. Check winter by storey

The winter method applies in climate zones 2–8 and assesses conductance relative to solar heat gain for each storey, including a mezzanine. It uses factors for orientation, room, floor and frame conditions. A colder-climate window cannot be assessed only by how much summer sun it blocks.

Read with the source

13.3.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 369. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Keep each storey and the factor inputs identifiable in the assessment.

4. Check summer with the actual openings

The summer method applies in climate zones 1–7 and compares aggregate solar heat gain with the storey allowance. Ventilation opening area and floor type affect the allowance table. The notes limit the design ventilation percentage attributed to a window because its frame obstructs part of the opening.

Read with the source

13.3.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 385. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Use the actual opening and floor arrangement in the inputs.

5. Shade the full glazing geometry

Permanent projecting shade must meet the stated side-extension or equivalent-reveal arrangement. Alternative external devices must restrict at least 80% of summer solar radiation and be readily operable if adjustable. Interior curtains are not automatically the external shading arrangement described by this clause.

Read with the source

13.3.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 397. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the sides and operating access as well as projection depth.

6. Treat substitutions as design changes

Changing frame colour, window size, orientation, floor finish or shading can change calculation inputs. A similar-looking product is not necessarily equivalent. Retain the original assessed values and obtain a revised check for the proposed combination instead of approving a substitution on a single marketing claim.

On the job

Record the revised product and its effect on the assessment.

Practice: For a simple geometry exercise, a projection P is 600 mm and height H is 1500 mm. What is P/H?
Worked answer: 0.4. 600 ÷ 1500 = 0.4. The actual measuring points must follow Figure 13.3.2b; this ratio alone is not the glazing assessment.
