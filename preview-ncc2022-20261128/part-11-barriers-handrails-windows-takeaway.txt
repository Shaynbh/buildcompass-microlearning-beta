Protect the edge and the opening
NCC 2022 Housing Provisions national text, 1 May 2023. Review draft.
Coverage: 11.3.1–11.3.8

1. Identify the fall trigger: A continuous barrier is required along the specified trafficable surfaces where a fall of 1 m or more is possible. Retaining walls have a particular access-path condition. Openable windows use their separate clauses. Read the operative wording carefully: exactly 1 m is included in the national barrier trigger.
Before applying these details, identify the relevant Volume Two pathway and read the state or territory schedule. Scope clauses identify the subjects covered; they do not make every detail applicable to every building. Use the defined terms and original figures where the provision depends on geometry.
Source: 11.3.1–11.3.2; 11.3.3

2. Measure the right barrier height: The general minimum is 1 m above a landing, deck or similar level surface and 865 mm above stair nosings or a ramp. A limited short inside-landing exception and transition detail are provided. Do not apply the stair height across an entire balcony simply because the stair connects to it.
Source: 11.3.4(1)–(3)

3. Control openings and climbing: General barrier openings must not pass a 125 mm sphere. A face-mounted barrier has an additional maximum 40 mm horizontal gap to the trafficable edge. Where the possible fall exceeds 4 m, elements between 150 and 760 mm above the floor must not facilitate climbing. Strength and glazing checks still apply.
Source: 11.3.4(4)–(11)

4. Provide a usable handhold: Required handrails run along at least one side, with their top at least 865 mm above the specified surface, and remain continuous subject to the listed exceptions. The general exception includes a change in elevation under 1 m. A broad barrier cap is not automatically a usable handrail simply because it is at the right height.
Source: 11.3.5

5. Treat wire barriers as tensioned systems: Wire-barrier tables depend on wire type, diameter, spacing, support spacing and tension or deflection. The unloaded gap alone is not enough because wires deflect. The explanatory notes identify the need to maintain tension over the service life. Use the complete table arrangement and keep commissioning measurements.
Source: 11.3.6

6. Separate bedroom and other window rules: Bedroom protection is triggered where the floor is at least 2 m above the surface beneath, with further conditions when the opening starts below 1.7 m above the floor. Devices or screens must restrict a 125 mm sphere and resist 250 N. Removable arrangements add child-resistant release and barrier conditions. Other rooms use the separate 4 m trigger.
Source: 11.3.7–11.3.8

Practice: A face-mounted barrier has a measured horizontal edge gap of 55 mm. How many mm above the 40 mm maximum is it?
Worked answer: 15. 55 − 40 = 15 mm. Check the actual measuring points and all other barrier requirements.

Further study: Complete wire tables and all window release/barrier scenarios need final visual and technical review.
