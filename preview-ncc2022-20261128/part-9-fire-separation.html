Keep the fire separation continuous

A fire-resisting wall is a continuous system. Its base, top, openings and services matter as much as the wall material.

Coverage: 9.1; 9.2.1–9.2.10; 9.3.1–9.3.4; 9.4.1–9.4.2: principal routes

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 11

1. Start with the separation distance

The national Class 1 external-wall trigger is less than 900 mm from the relevant boundary or less than 1.8 m from another building on the same allotment, with specified exceptions. Distances are measured at right angles under the clause. South Australia has variations, so the national geometry must not be applied without the applicable schedule.

Before applying these details, identify the relevant Volume Two pathway and read the state or territory schedule. Scope clauses identify the subjects covered; they do not make every detail applicable to every building. Use the defined terms and original figures where the provision depends on geometry.

9.1.1–9.1.2; 9.2.1–9.2.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 201. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Identify the boundary type and associated buildings before measuring.

2. Choose the external-wall system

The external-wall route includes an FRL of 60/60/60 tested from outside, or the specified minimum 90 mm masonry or masonry-veneer alternatives. The wall must start and terminate as required. A selected fire-rated sheet does not establish a whole wall system, and stopping short at a combustible eaves junction can interrupt the separation.

Read with the source

9.2.3(1)–(2). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 205. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check footing-to-roof continuity and the tested assembly details.

3. Treat openings deliberately

Openings in a required fire-resisting external wall need the stated protection, including the specified fire-window or self-closing solid-core-door options. Limited non-habitable-room exceptions depend on setback, opening area and construction. Do not treat every bathroom window as exempt merely because the room is not habitable.

Read with the source

9.2.3(3)–(5). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 205. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record the exact exception, dimensions and construction if one is used.

4. Include garages in the fire path

An associated Class 10a building can form a route for fire between a boundary and a dwelling, or between dwellings. The alternative protective layouts in 9.2.5–9.2.7 must be selected against the actual arrangement. A garage not directly centred between two buildings can still affect separation.

Read with the source

9.2.4–9.2.7. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 207. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Draw the whole site arrangement, not just the nearest wall.

5. Separate dwellings through the roof

Separating walls between Class 1 buildings have a different route from an external boundary wall. Their construction, roof junction, permitted crossings, penetrations and nearby projections must remain coordinated. Services need the specific tested or prescribed treatment in 9.3.2; ordinary expanding foam is not automatically a qualifying fire-stopping system.

Read with the source

9.3.1–9.3.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 229. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Inspect service and roof junctions before concealment.

6. Check an unrelated garage above or below

Part 9.4 addresses a dwelling over or under a private garage not associated with it. It specifies separating wall and floor alternatives, support requirements and preservation of fire performance at penetrations. NSW has a Part 9.4 variation. Do not transfer the detail for an ordinary attached garage without establishing which dwelling the garage serves.

Read with the source

9.4.1–9.4.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 234. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record ownership association and the vertical building arrangement.

7. Check a carport exception against every condition

The national open-carport exception requires at least two open sides and at least one-third of its perimeter open, with a defined 500 mm clearance for a side to count as open. Roof, lining and interface conditions also apply. A vehicle door does not count as an open side. South Australia has a variation.

Read with the source

9.2.8. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 222. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Use the complete clause and interface figure before claiming the exemption.

8. Check projections and roof lights separately

Encroachments within the separation space have a defined list of permitted arrangements. Combustible roof lights in a roof required to have non-combustible covering are limited to 20% aggregate area and the specified separation distances. Do not assume a compliant wall setback automatically permits any eave, projection or skylight.

Read with the source

9.2.9–9.2.10. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 224. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Mark projections and roof lights on the same fire-separation plan.

Practice: A relevant boundary setback is 750 mm. How much less is it than the 900 mm national trigger distance?
Worked answer: 150. 900 − 750 = 150 mm. Check the full scope, measurement method and jurisdiction before choosing protection.
