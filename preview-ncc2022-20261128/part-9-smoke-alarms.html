Put the alarm where it can do its job

Product compliance, location, power and interconnection are separate checks. A box on the ceiling is not the whole alarm strategy.

Coverage: 9.5.1–9.5.5

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 9

1. Identify the building class

Class 1a dwellings and Class 1b accommodation do not have identical alarm-location requirements. Start by identifying the actual classification and applicable jurisdiction. NSW has a variation flagged in the national source, and local requirements may add to this teaching example. Do not substitute a rental-property checklist for the NCC pathway without checking both.

Read with the source

9.5.1–9.5.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 236. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record class and jurisdiction with the alarm layout.

2. Check the product and supply

Smoke alarms are required to comply with AS 3786 under this national provision. Where the building has a consumer mains supply, the alarms use that supply. Product selection and electrical installation are separate from deciding the number of alarms. Keep the product and installation evidence with the handover information.

Read with the source

9.5.1. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 236. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Confirm the actual product and supply arrangement.

3. Connect the alarms within the dwelling

Where more than one alarm is installed, they must be interconnected. The explanatory information applies this within a single dwelling rather than requiring alarms in neighbouring dwellings to sound together. Confirm the connection is functional and compatible with the chosen products instead of assuming two independent alarms provide the same warning.

Read with the source

9.5.1(d). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 236. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Include interconnection in the commissioning record.

4. Cover bedrooms and other storeys

For Class 1a, locate alarms in bedroom corridors or hallways, or between bedrooms and the rest of the building when there is no such corridor, and on other storeys without bedrooms. Class 1b also requires alarms in each bedroom. A split-level or separated bedroom arrangement needs its actual layout checked.

Read with the source

9.5.2–9.5.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 236. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Trace the route from every bedroom rather than counting floors alone.

5. Keep the alarm out of dead air

Alarm location is constrained by ceiling and wall geometry. The national details include clearance from corners and a separate location range below the apex of a sloping ceiling. Wall mounting is a conditional option where ceiling mounting is not possible. Follow the clause and compatible product instructions rather than choosing the easiest cable location.

Read with the source

9.5.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 238. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check ceiling shape and the product installation detail.

6. Resolve special locations and evacuation lighting

Where a private garage is likely to cause spurious smoke signals, the national clause permits a suitable alternative alarm under AS 1670.1 while retaining smoke alarms elsewhere in the Class 1 building. Class 1b evacuation lighting has its own activation requirements. Neither exception is a reason to remove warning from the building.

Read with the source

9.5.1(b); 9.5.5. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 236. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record why a special arrangement applies and how it is tested.

8. Check your understanding

1. Can the Class 1a layout automatically be used for Class 1b?

2. Does a battery-only product automatically meet this mains-supplied building provision?

3. Are two unrelated standalone alarms equivalent to an interconnected pair?

4. Does one alarm on the ground floor automatically cover upstairs bedrooms?

5. Is the highest point of every ceiling automatically the right position?

6. Can a nuisance alarm simply be disconnected as the final solution?

Practice: A fictional Class 1b layout has four bedrooms and one bedroom corridor. Counting only those listed locations, how many alarms are indicated?
Worked answer: 5. 4 + 1 = 5. Other storeys, geometry, jurisdiction and the complete layout may add requirements.
