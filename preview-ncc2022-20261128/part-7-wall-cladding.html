Finish the wall without trapping water

Cladding is a system of boards or sheets, fixings, joints, flashings and clearances. The edges deserve as much attention as the middle.

Coverage: 7.5.1–7.5.8

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 9

1. Identify the cladding material

Part 7.5 contains particular timber, fibre-cement, hardboard and plywood arrangements. It is not approval for every proprietary facade. Board and sheet products use different support and fixing rules. Confirm material, thickness, wind classification and the relevant installation pathway before applying a familiar detail.

7.5.1–7.5.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 176. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record the exact product and the applicable table.

2. Timber profiles manage their own laps

Splayed weatherboard laps differ by timber type. The stated minimums include 30 mm for hardwood, cypress and treated pine, 20 mm for western red cedar and 25 mm for Baltic pine. Tongue-and-groove boards laid horizontally or diagonally have the tongue uppermost, and orientation affects the sarking requirement.

Read with the source

7.5.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 176. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Inspect the chosen profile before setting the first course.

3. Board widths affect fixings

Fibre-cement and hardboard planks have their own minimum thicknesses and lap requirements. Boards up to 130 mm wide use one fixing at each stud in the stated arrangement; wider boards use two. The fixing position must avoid unintentionally fastening through the thin edge of the board below.

Read with the source

7.5.3. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 180. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check width, thickness and fixing position together.

4. Sheets and soffits need the right table

Sheet wall cladding and eaves or soffit linings have separate tables for supports and fasteners. Edge distances, wind class and sheet thickness remain part of the selection. A soffit exposed to wind is not merely decorative lining; changing its support spacing needs a supported installation detail.

Read with the source

7.5.4–7.5.5. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 181. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check sheet edges and intermediate supports before covering joints.

5. Drain the opening flashings

Flashings are required around openings subject to the clause and its eaves exception. They must discharge outside the cladding. The detail includes lap, fixing and extension requirements, with extension beyond reveals where practicable. A concealed flashing that turns water into the wall is not fixed by a neat external sealant bead.

Read with the source

7.5.6. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 184. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Follow the water path at the head, jamb and sill.

6. Maintain ground and top clearances

Ground clearance varies with conditions: the national provision distinguishes low-rainfall or sandy well-drained sites, suitably sloping impermeable surfaces and other situations. Other termite and drainage requirements still apply. The cladding-bottom and capping details must be coordinated with the floor framing, membrane and adjacent finished levels.

Practice: Six equal board exposures are 150 mm each. What is their combined vertical coverage in mm?
Worked answer: 900. 6 × 150 = 900 mm. Exposure is not the same as full board width; the selected lap must also be allowed for.
