Make every step predictable

People expect the next step to behave like the last. Finished dimensions, consistency and grip matter together.

Coverage: 11.1; 11.2.1–11.2.6

National Housing Provisions text dated 1 May 2023. Confirm the approval edition, relevant Volume Two pathway, state or territory variations and referenced standards for the project. General education, not accredited CPD or individual compliance advice.

Section 1 of 10

1. Measure the finished geometry

A flight normally has between two and eighteen risers. Riser and going dimensions are measured using the clause figures, not simply the timber tread depth. Floor coverings at the top and bottom can alter the first and last risers, so calculate the finished levels before fabricating or accepting the stair.

Before applying these details, identify the relevant Volume Two pathway and read the state or territory schedule. Scope clauses identify the subjects covered; they do not make every detail applicable to every building. Use the defined terms and original figures where the provision depends on geometry.

Read with the source

11.1.1–11.1.2; 11.2.1–11.2.2. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 289. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Measure from finished surfaces and record the first and last risers.

2. Check all three limits

For stairs other than spiral stairs, the national table gives risers from 115 to 190 mm, goings from 240 to 355 mm and a slope relationship 2R + G from 550 to 700 mm. Passing the slope relationship alone does not excuse a riser or going outside its individual limits.

Read with the source

Table 11.2.2a. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 290. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check R, G and 2R + G separately.

3. Keep the flight consistent

The allowed variation is no more than 5 mm between adjacent risers or goings and no more than 10 mm between the largest and smallest in a flight. Open risers must not allow a 125 mm sphere through under the general rule. Checking only an average can hide a dangerous outlier.

Read with the source

11.2.2(1)(c)–(d). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 290. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record every riser and going rather than one representative step.

4. Use special stair rules only in scope

Infrequently used non-habitable-room stairs, spiral stairs and winders have specific alternatives. Winders are limited in number and measured at defined positions. An attic used every day cannot be assumed to fit an infrequent-use exception merely because the drawing calls it an attic.

Read with the source

11.2.2(2)–(5). Open official NCC 2022 source, page 290. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Record the actual use and the applicable measurement line.

5. Coordinate ramps and landings

The general external-doorway or internal ramp limit is 1:8, with landings at top, bottom and intervals no more than 15 m. Landings generally have at least 750 mm length and a gradient no steeper than 1:50. Livable housing and alpine requirements can require different arrangements.

Read with the source

11.2.3; 11.2.5. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 296. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the applicable pathway before using the general ramp limit.

6. Select grip for wet or dry use

Slip-resistance classes depend on the surface and whether it is expected to become wet. The table distinguishes ramps, tread surfaces and nosing or landing-edge strips. A dry internal test classification is not automatically suitable for an exposed external stair. Obtain the classification for the installed finish.

Read with the source

11.2.4. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 296. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Keep the test classification with the finish specification.

7. Resolve the doorway threshold

Where a doorway threshold is more than 230 mm above the adjoining surface, the national provision requires steps with riser and going dimensions in accordance with 11.2.2. This is read with the separate landing and applicable livable-housing provisions. A single oversized drop is not made acceptable by calling it a threshold.

Read with the source

11.2.6. Open official NCC 2022 source, page 298. Use the complete clause, figure, table notes and scope together.

On the job

Check the final paving and floor finishes at each external door.

9. Check your understanding

1. Is tread depth always the going?

2. Does a passing 2R + G value excuse a 200 mm ordinary stair riser?

3. Can an average measurement prove every step is consistent?

4. Does an attic label automatically permit the infrequent-use stair table?

5. Is 1:8 automatically suitable for a livable-housing access path?

6. Can a dry-only selection be assumed suitable for weather exposure?

7. Can a 250 mm drop be ignored as merely a threshold under this clause?

Practice: For an ordinary stair with R = 175 mm and G = 270 mm, calculate 2R + G in mm.
Worked answer: 620. 2 × 175 + 270 = 620 mm. The individual values also fit the general table; consistency, structure and other details remain separate.
